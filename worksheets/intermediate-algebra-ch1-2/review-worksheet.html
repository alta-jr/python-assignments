Part A — Isolation§1.1

  1. Solve for x:   2x − 13 − x + 45 = 2
  2. Solve for x:   √3x + 1 = x − 3.   Check each candidate in the original equation.
  3. Solve for x:   3x − 2 = 5x + 4.   State any values of x that must be excluded.
  4. Solve for x:   | 2x − 5 | = 7
  5. Solve for x in terms of a and b:   xa + xb = 1.   What must be true of a and b?
  6. The simple-interest formula is A = P(1 + rt). Solve it for t.

Part B — Substitution & Elimination§1.2–1.3

  1. Solve by substitution:   y = 2x − 3  and  3x + 2y = 8
  2. Solve by elimination:   4x − 3y = 18  and  3x + 5y = −1
  3. Solve the system:   x + y + z = 6,   2x − y + 3z = 9,   x − 2y + 4z = 9
  4. Solve, or explain why no solution exists:   2x − 6y = 5  and  −3x + 9y = 1.   What do the two lines look like?
  5. Solve the system:   x2 + y2 = 25  and  y = x + 1.   Describe the graphs and what the solutions mean.
  6. Solve:   x + y = 5,   y + z = 8,   z + x = 9.   Adding all three equations is faster than substituting.

Part C — Lines & Graphing§1.4

  1. Find the equation of the line through (−2, 5) and (4, −7) in slope-intercept form, then give both intercepts.
  2. Find the intersection of  2x + 3y = 12  and  y = x − 1  algebraically, then sketch both lines to confirm.
  3. Consider the line 3x − 4y = 8.
    1. Rewrite it in slope-intercept form and state the slope.
    2. Find the line parallel to it through (0, −1).
    3. Find the line perpendicular to it through (0, −1).
  4. A theater sells 250 tickets and collects $2,300. Adult tickets cost $12 and student tickets cost $7. How many of each were sold? Define your variables before you write equations.
  5. A rectangle has perimeter 54 cm, and its length is 3 cm more than twice its width. Find its dimensions.

Part D — Function Basics§2.1

  1. Find the domain of each function. Write your answer in interval notation.
    1. f(x) = x − 3x2 − 4x − 5
    2. g(x) = √7 − 2x
    3. h(x) = 3√x + 4
  2. Let f(x) = 2x2 − x + 1. Compute and simplify f(−3), f(a + 1), and f(−x).
  3. Find the range of f(x) = x2 − 4x + 7 by completing the square. Justify your answer.
  4. For f(x) = x2 − 5x, simplify the difference quotient f(x + h) − f(x)h  (h ≠ 0).
  5. Which of these define y as a function of x? Explain each answer.
    1. {(1, 2), (2, 4), (3, 2)}
    2. {(1, 2), (1, 3), (2, 5)}
    3. x = y2
    4. y = x2

Part E — Graphs of Functions§2.2

  1. Starting from y = | x |, list in order the transformations that produce y = −2| x − 3 | + 4. Give the vertex, say which way it opens, and sketch it.
  2. The graph of y = f(x) passes through (2, −5). Give a point that must lie on each graph below.
    1. y = f(x − 4) + 1
    2. y = −3f(x)
    3. y = f(2x)
    4. y = f(−x)
  3. Sketch y = √x + 2 − 3. State its domain, its range, and the coordinates of its endpoint.
  4. Classify each as even, odd, or neither, and justify algebraically using f(−x).
    1. f(x) = x3 − 4x
    2. g(x) = x4 − 3x2 + 1
    3. h(x) = x2 + x

Part F — Composition§2.3

  1. Let f(x) = 2x − 1 and g(x) = x2 + 3. Find and simplify f(g(x)), g(f(x)), f(g(2)), and g(f(2)).
  2. Let f(x) = 1x − 2 and g(x) = √x. Find (f ∘ g)(x) and its domain. Two separate restrictions apply.
  3. Write h(x) = (3x + 1)5 as a composition f(g(x)) of two simpler functions, neither of which is x itself.
  4. Let f(x) = 2x − 3. Find f(f(x)) and f(f(f(x))). Predict the pattern for four applications.

Part G — Inverse Functions§2.4

  1. Find f−1(x) for f(x) = 2x − 53, then verify that f(f−1(x)) = x.
  2. Find f−1(x) for f(x) = 4 − x3.
  3. Find f−1(x) for f(x) = x + 1x − 3, and state the domain of f−1.
  4. Let f(x) = x2 − 6x + 5 with domain restricted to x ≥ 3. Find f−1(x) and its domain. Complete the square first.
  5. Answer each in a sentence.
    1. f is invertible and f(3) = 8. What is f−1(8)?
    2. What is (f−1 ∘ f)(5)?
    3. Why does g(x) = x2 have no inverse on all real numbers?
    4. How is the graph of f−1 related to the graph of f?

Challenge Problems — optional★

  1. Find all real solutions of  √x + 5 + √x = 5.
  2. Let f(x) = 2x + 3x − 2. Show that f(f(x)) = x for every x in the domain. What does that tell you about f−1?
  3. A function satisfies f(x) + 2f( 1x ) = 3x for every x ≠ 0. Find f(2), then find f(x) in general.
  4. Find the range of f(x) = 2x + 1x − 3 without graphing. Solve y = f(x) for x and ask which y are impossible.
